Mortgage Broker Gold Coast Cost Guide Explained

Figuring out what a broker costs starts with how they are paid. The published guidance says most residential borrowers pay no direct broker fee, because the lender usually pays the broker when the loan settles. For more complex or commercial lending, any borrower-paid fee should be disclosed in writing before work proceeds.

The same page cites typical commission figures: upfront commission around 0.65 to 0.70 per cent of the loan amount, and ongoing trail commission around 0.15 per cent per year. Those numbers describe the industry model rather than a quote, so treat them as context when you ask a broker how they are remunerated on your loan. That model is why the useful question is not only the rate, but whether the broker can explain lender fit, costs and timing clearly. Costs beyond the broker fee

Broker remuneration is only one line item. The trade-offs between interest rate, comparison rate, repayment type and loan features shape the total cost, and a lower headline rate can come with fewer features or different fees.

  • Deposit size: a smaller deposit can mean Lenders Mortgage Insurance, so ask how the premium changes the loan amount.
  • Low deposit pathway: eligible buyers are cited as having a 5 per cent deposit pathway worth checking against current rules.
  • Grants: the page cites a $30,000 Queensland First Home Owner Grant for eligible new builds under $750,000, current to 30 June 2026. Verify it against current government rules before relying on it in a contract budget.
  • Property type: a Coomera house-and-land package may need construction or progress-payment details, and a Southport apartment can affect the lender security assessment, which can flow through to cost.

A grant can improve cash flow, but lender approval still depends on the borrower, the property and the loan structure, so treat every concession as a checklist item rather than a guarantee.

How the Best Interests Duty shapes value

Cost is not only dollars. Under ASIC rules a mortgage broker must act under the Best Interests Duty when recommending a loan. Make that duty practical by asking why the recommended lender is suitable, which alternatives were considered, and whether a lower repayment involves a feature trade-off.

If a recommendation seems cheaper, ask what was given up to get there. A lower repayment can mean offset access, flexibility or features traded away. Accreditation also gives a standards framework: the page refers to MFAA-accredited brokers and FBAA-accredited specialists. Membership does not replace reading the credit proposal, but it signals the broker works within an industry code.

Cost questions to ask before you apply

The described process starts with a short enquiry and says no documents are needed up front. Before a full application the broker still needs income, expenses, deposit, liabilities, property goal and preferred repayment style, because good preparation stops a comparison being built on missing details. Take this mortgage broker gold coast cost checklist into that first conversation:

  1. State whether the goal is purchase, refinance, investment, pre-approval, low deposit lending, bridging or a self-employed application.
  2. List current debts, regular commitments and the deposit or equity available.
  3. Ask for the gap between the interest rate and the comparison rate on shortlisted loans.
  4. Ask whether offset, redraw, fixed or variable features change the recommendation.
  5. Request written disclosure of any broker fee, lender commission and key loan assumptions before applying.

When timing changes the cost

A borrower selling and buying with different settlement dates should ask whether bridging finance is suitable and what repayments may look like while both properties are involved. Timing can also affect grant eligibility, since the cited Queensland grant runs to a stated date, so contract timing matters as much as the rate.
